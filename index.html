Purpose and scope

This policy governs how the FL UAS MEDNET Working Group collects, uses, stores, shares, retains, and disposes of information submitted through stakeholder intake forms, surveys, meeting registrations, and related coordination tools. It is intended to support responsible collaboration while limiting privacy risk through data minimization, purpose limitation, access controls, and secure handling practices.

This policy applies to information collected directly by the Working Group or on its behalf by convening organizations, contractors, vendors, consultants, or survey-platform providers. It covers online forms, questionnaires, spreadsheets, email-based responses, registration lists, and any exported or compiled datasets created from those sources.

Information collected

The Working Group should collect only the information reasonably necessary to identify potential stakeholders, understand organizational capabilities, coordinate participation, and support planning for meetings, pilots, and working-group activities.

Information collected may include:

  • Organization name, website, and sector.
  • Respondent name, title, business email, and business phone number.
  • Geographic service area, infrastructure assets, operational interests, and partnership interests.
  • Survey responses about technical capabilities, regulatory interests, research interests, medical logistics interests, and pilot participation preferences.
  • Administrative metadata generated by the survey platform, such as submission date and time, if retained by the platform.

The Working Group should avoid collecting Social Security numbers, driver license numbers, bank or payment data, personal health details, patient data, insurance identifiers, precise personal geolocation, usernames with passwords, or any other sensitive personal information unless a separate written justification, notice, and control structure are established in advance.

Permitted uses

Information collected through intake forms and surveys may be used only for legitimate Working Group purposes. Those purposes include stakeholder mapping, meeting coordination, working-group communications, pilot planning, infrastructure and capability analysis, partnership development, regulatory planning, and preparation of de-identified summaries, reports, or presentations.

The Working Group may use responses to segment stakeholders by role, region, interest area, or technical capability in order to organize outreach and planning. Any public-facing summary should present aggregated or de-identified information unless a participant has expressly agreed to attribution.

Information collected through intake forms and surveys must not be sold, licensed for unrelated commercial use, or used for unrelated marketing. Information also must not be used for any purpose materially inconsistent with the notice provided at the time of collection.

Voluntary participation and notice

Participation in Working Group intake forms and surveys is voluntary. Individuals should be told, at or before the point of collection, the general purpose of the form, the categories of information being requested, how the information will be used, whether responses may be shared within the consortium, and whom to contact with questions.

Each form or survey should contain a short privacy notice stating that submission is voluntary and that information will be used for Working Group coordination, planning, and related communications. If a future survey has a broader or materially different purpose, a revised notice should be provided before collection.

Sharing and disclosure

Access to identifiable responses should be limited to authorized Working Group leads and designated administrative or analytical support personnel with a need to know. If survey or intake operations are supported by a contractor, consultant, or platform provider, that party should be contractually limited to using the information only for Working Group purposes and should be required to implement reasonable security safeguards.

Identifiable data may be shared internally among convening organizations only to the extent necessary for stakeholder engagement, scheduling, analysis, and pilot planning. External sharing of identifiable data with third parties should occur only with participant permission, where required to fulfill a Working Group purpose clearly disclosed in advance, or where required by law.

Reports, slide decks, stakeholder maps, and planning memoranda should use aggregated or de-identified information whenever practical. Contact lists should not be distributed broadly unless the individuals listed have agreed to be included in a shared directory or the disclosure is otherwise necessary and appropriate for the Working Group's stated purpose.

Security safeguards

The Working Group and any service providers should take reasonable measures to protect data in electronic form, consistent with the Florida Information Protection Act requirement that covered entities and third-party agents protect personal information with reasonable safeguards.

Reasonable safeguards should include:

  • Role-based access controls for form responses and exported files.
  • Multi-factor authentication on primary administrator accounts where available.
  • Encrypted transmission for web-based forms and encrypted storage where feasible.
  • Restricting download, forwarding, and local duplication of identifiable response files to those who need access.
  • Maintaining current software, access reviews, and revocation of access when a team member no longer needs it.
  • Using platforms and settings that avoid unnecessary third-party tracking where possible.

If a third-party platform is used, administrators should review default settings for data retention, account permissions, link-sharing, and embedded tracking. Web-based survey collection should favor secure transmission methods and avoid unnecessary persistent tracking technologies.

Data quality and minimization

The Working Group should periodically review form questions to ensure that only necessary fields are collected. Optional fields should be clearly labeled, and free-text questions should be used carefully because respondents may disclose more information than intended.

If responses are compiled into master stakeholder lists or analytical summaries, the Working Group should take reasonable steps to ensure records are accurate, current, and relevant to the purposes for which they are maintained. Participants should be given a practical way to update their contact information or correct material errors.

Retention and disposal

Identifiable intake and survey information should be retained only for as long as reasonably necessary to support Working Group planning, follow-up communications, pilot development, and recordkeeping needs. As a default working standard, identifiable intake and survey responses should be reviewed at least annually and deleted, archived, or de-identified when no longer needed for active coordination.

When records containing personal information are no longer needed, the Working Group or its service providers should dispose of them securely by deleting, erasing, shredding, or otherwise rendering the information unreadable or undecipherable, consistent with Florida statutory requirements for disposal of records containing personal information.

Sensitive health information and HIPAA boundary

The Working Group's intake forms and general stakeholder surveys are not intended to collect patient records or protected health information. HIPAA generally applies to covered entities and business associates handling protected health information, not to ordinary stakeholder surveys that collect professional contact information or organizational capability data alone.

If a future FL UAS MEDNET activity involves patient-linked data, clinical case information, or identifiable health information provided by a covered entity, that activity should be governed under a separate clinical data policy, appropriate legal agreements, and any applicable HIPAA or data-use requirements before collection or sharing begins.

Incident response

Any suspected unauthorized access, disclosure, loss, or misuse of identifiable intake or survey data should be reported promptly to the designated Working Group privacy or administrative lead. The incident should be documented, access should be contained, and affected systems or accounts should be secured without delay.

If an incident involves personal information in electronic form, the Working Group and any relevant service providers should evaluate whether notification obligations are triggered under the Florida Information Protection Act, including notice to affected individuals and, where applicable, notice to the Florida Department of Legal Affairs.

Participant rights and contact

Participants should be able to request correction of basic contact information, ask questions about how their responses are used, or request that future Working Group communications stop. Where practical and lawful, the Working Group should honor requests to remove an individual from distribution lists or to stop using identifiable response data for future outreach.

Each intake form or survey should identify a point of contact for privacy or data-use questions. For FL UAS MEDNET, that contact can be the designated administrative lead identified on the form or invitation materials.

Suggested short notice for forms

By submitting this form, you agree that FL UAS MEDNET may use the information provided to evaluate stakeholder interest, coordinate Working Group activities, support pilot and infrastructure planning, and communicate about related meetings and opportunities. Submission is voluntary. Please do not include patient information, medical records, passwords, financial account information, or other sensitive personal data in this form. Identifiable responses will be shared only with authorized Working Group personnel and service providers supporting this effort, and aggregated or de-identified information may be used in planning materials and reports.
